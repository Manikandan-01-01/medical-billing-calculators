Key takeaways

  • A 90-day global covers 92 days: the day before surgery, the day of surgery and 90 days after.
  • A 10-day global covers 11 days: the day of surgery and 10 days after.
  • A 000 code covers the day of the procedure only.
  • Modifiers 24, 25, 57, 58, 78, 79 and FT mark services Medicare can pay separately during a global period.

What is a global period?

A global period is the span of days around a surgery when related care is paid as part of the surgery. Medicare calls this the global surgical package. It covers pre-op visits after the decision to operate, the surgery, related post-op visits, complications that do not need a return to the operating room, and routine items like dressing changes and suture removal (Claims Processing Manual, Ch. 12, §40.1).

Each code’s global days come from the CMS physician fee schedule. This Medicare global period calculator reads them straight from the CMS 2026 relative value file.

How do you calculate a global period?

Count from the date of surgery. For a major surgery (090), CMS counts 1 day before surgery, the day of surgery and the 90 days after it. For a minor procedure (010), CMS counts the day of surgery and the 10 days after it. CMS gives this example: surgery on January 5 has a pre-op day of January 4 and a last post-op day of April 5. A 10-day procedure on January 5 ends January 15 (Ch. 12, §40.1.E).

The global surgery calculator does this date math for you. It handles month ends and leap years. For example, a 90-day global from January 5, 2028 ends April 4, 2028, one day earlier than in 2026.

What is not included in the global surgery package?

Medicare pays these services separately, even during a global period (Ch. 12, §40.1.B):

  • The first evaluation that leads to the decision for a major surgery. For minor procedures, that evaluation is always included.
  • Visits unrelated to the diagnosis for the surgery, unless they are for complications.
  • Treatment of the underlying condition, and diagnostic tests and procedures.
  • Clearly distinct procedures that are not re-operations or treatment for complications. A new post-op period begins.
  • Treatment for complications that needs a return trip to the operating room.
  • Critical care (99291, 99292) unrelated to the surgery.

When a post-op visit denies as bundled, I check two things first: the end date of the global period and the diagnosis on the visit. A bundled visit often comes back as CO-97.

Which modifiers apply during the global period?

Medicare pays a visit or procedure in the global period only when the right modifier shows why it is separate. Without 24, 25, 57 or FT, MACs deny E/M services on the day of surgery or in the post-op period (Ch. 12, §40.4).

Modifier 24 is for an unrelated E/M visit during the post-op period. Modifier 79 is for an unrelated procedure during the post-op period, and it starts a new global period (Ch. 12, §40.2.A.7).

These rules come from Claims Processing Manual Ch. 12, §40.2 and the CMS MLN Global Surgery booklet (MLN907166). The calculator shows only the modifiers that fit the code’s global days.

How is split global care paid with modifiers 54, 55 and 56?

CMS lists a pre-op, intra-op and post-op percentage for each 010 and 090 code. A surgeon who bills 54 gets the pre-op and intra-op share. The doctor who bills 55 gets the post-op share. For 27447, total knee arthroplasty, the file lists 10% pre-op, 69% intra-op and 21% post-op. So 54 pays 79% and 55 pays 21% of the fee schedule amount (Ch. 12, §40.4.B).

Both claims use the same code and the date of surgery. Each doctor keeps the written transfer agreement. Split care does not apply to 000 codes. To see the dollar amount, use the RVU Calculator and multiply the payment by the share.

Frequently asked questions

Is this global period calculator free?

Yes. It is free and needs no sign-up. The code and date you enter stay in your browser. The tool downloads the CMS data file and does the date math on your device.

Does the day of surgery count in the global period?

Yes. For a 10-day global, CMS counts the day of surgery and the 10 days after it, so the total is 11 days. For a 90-day global, CMS also counts the day before surgery, for a total of 92 days.

When does a 90-day global period end?

It ends 90 days after the day of surgery. CMS gives the example of surgery on January 5: the pre-op day is January 4 and the last day of the post-op period is April 5.

Do global surgery rules apply to other doctors?

The global package covers the surgeon and members of the same group with the same specialty. Services of other physicians are not included, except when the surgeon and the other physician agree on a transfer of care.

Do commercial payers use the same global periods?

Not always. Commercial payers set their own policies. Check the payer’s manual or contract. The calculator uses Medicare’s CMS global days.

Will global days change in 2027?

They can. CMS publishes the global days for every code in its yearly relative value file. We will load the 2027 file when CMS posts it.
