Key takeaways

  • An MUE is the most units of a code Medicare expects for one patient on one day.
  • MAI 1 checks each claim line. MAI 2 and MAI 3 add up all lines for the day.
  • MAI 2 is an absolute limit. MAI 3 can pay on appeal with records.
  • You cannot bill the patient for units denied by an MUE, even with an ABN.

What is an MUE in medical billing?

An MUE is the maximum units of service for a HCPCS or CPT code that the same provider reports for the same patient on the same date of service, on the vast majority of correct claims. MUE stands for Medically Unlikely Edit. Medicare contractors use MUEs to deny units above that limit (NCCI Policy Manual 2026, Ch. 1, §V).

The MUE units checker looks up your code in the CMS practitioner table, compares your units with the limit, and shows the MUE value, adjudication indicator and rationale together.

What does the MUE adjudication indicator (MAI) mean?

The MUE Adjudication Indicator (MAI) is the CMS field that tells you how Medicare counts units against the MUE. MAI 1 is a claim line edit. MAI 2 and MAI 3 are date of service edits (NCCI Policy Manual 2026, Ch. 1, §V).

Most codes are date of service edits, and only 41 codes in the practitioner table use the claim line indicator, MAI 1.

How are units counted against an MUE?

Units are compared line by line for MAI 1 and summed for the whole day for MAI 2 or 3. If the units exceed the MUE, all units on that line or that day deny, not just the extra ones (NCCI Policy Manual 2026, Ch. 1, §V).

For example, 96372 has an MUE of 4 with MAI 3. Two lines of 3 and 2 units add up to 5, so all 5 units deny. A0425 has an MUE of 250 with MAI 1, so lines of 200 and 100 each pass on their own.

Some MACs let you bill a repeated service over a date span on one line. The contractor divides the units by the days in the span and rounds to the nearest whole number. That number is compared with the MUE.

What happens when a claim exceeds the MUE?

The units deny as a coding denial, and you can appeal to the MAC. For MAI 1 or 3, the MAC can pay correctly coded, medically necessary units above the MUE. For MAI 2, an override would be contrary to CMS policy (NCCI Policy Manual 2026, Introduction and Ch. 1).

An ABN does not help. CMS says an ABN is not appropriate for an MUE denial, and the patient cannot be billed for the denied units. When a code denies often, the manual asks you to check 4 things: the code choice, how units are counted, medical necessity, and why your pattern differs from national data. Frequently asked questions

Is this MUE lookup free?

Yes. It is free and needs no sign-up. The code and units you enter stay in your browser.

Which MUE table does the lookup use?

It uses the CMS Medicare practitioner services MUE table effective October 1, 2026. The outpatient hospital and DME supplier tables are separate and are not included.

Can I bill more units than the MUE?

Sometimes. With MAI 1, a truly separate service can go on its own line with a modifier. With MAI 3, the MAC can pay extra units on appeal when records support them. With MAI 2, units above the MUE are not payable.

Can I bill the patient for units denied by an MUE?

No. An MUE denial is a coding denial. An ABN does not shift liability, and the provider cannot bill the patient for those units.

Why does a code show no MUE?

Not every code has an MUE, and CMS keeps some MUE values confidential. Check the code is valid and check payer policy before you bill extra units.

How often do MUE values change?

CMS updates the MUE tables every quarter, on the same schedule as the NCCI PTP edits. The next table is effective January 1, 2027, and we will load it then.
