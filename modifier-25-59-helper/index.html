Key takeaways

  • Modifier 25 goes on an E/M code. Modifier 59 never does.
  • Use XE, XP, XS or XU instead of 59 whenever one fits.
  • Different code descriptors or different diagnoses do not justify 59.
  • No modifier bypasses an NCCI edit with indicator 0.

What is the difference between modifier 25 and 59?

Modifier 25 is for a significant, separately identifiable E/M service on the same day as a procedure. Modifier 59 is for a distinct procedure or service, other than an E/M, that is not normally reported with another one on the same day. So 25 goes on the E/M code, and 59 goes on a procedure code (NCCI Policy Manual 2026, Ch. 1).

Both are NCCI PTP-associated modifiers. Both need a medical record that supports them. The modifier 59 vs 25 question usually comes down to one test: is the service an E/M visit or not?

What do modifiers XE, XS, XP and XU mean?

The X{EPSU} modifiers are 4 more specific versions of modifier 59, effective January 1, 2015. CMS asks you to use them instead of 59 whenever one fits (CMS MLN1783722).

When is modifier 25 correct?

Modifier 25 is correct when the E/M goes above and beyond the usual work of a minor or XXX procedure. The global period of the procedure decides which modifier, if any, the E/M can carry (NCCI Policy Manual 2026, Ch. 1).

The diagnosis can be the same for both services. A new patient alone does not justify the E/M. Medicare also does not pay G2211 with an E/M billed with modifier 25, except on the same day as an AWV, a vaccine administration or a Part B preventive service (CMS MLN006764). Check the visit level in the E/M level calculator before you add modifier 25.

When should you use XS instead of 59?

Use XS when the 2 procedures were on different organs or different anatomic regions and no anatomic modifier fits. Anatomic modifiers come first. Procedures on opposite sides of the body get RT and LT, not 59 or XS (CMS MLN1783722).

For example, CMS says 11102 and 17000 can take 59 or XS when they were at different sites on the same side of the body. Contiguous structures in one organ or region are a single site. The nail, nail bed and nearby soft tissue of one toe are one site, and so are the retina and choroid of one eye.

When do 59, XE and XU apply in the same encounter?

CMS allows 3 limited cases where 2 services in the same encounter are separate because they are separated in time and do not overlap (CMS MLN1783722):

  • Timed codes in separate time blocks: use 59 or XE. For example, 10 minutes of 97140, 15 minutes of 97750, then 5 more minutes of 97140.
  • A diagnostic test that led to the treatment: use 59 or XU, when the test came first, decided the treatment and was not otherwise required.
  • A diagnostic test after the treatment ended: use 59 or XU, when it was not a required, common or expected follow-up.

What are the most common modifier 59 mistakes?

The most common mistake is using 59 because the 2 codes describe different procedures. The codes in an edit pair usually describe different procedures, so that alone never justifies 59 or XU (NCCI Policy Manual 2026, Ch. 1). Other mistakes include:

  • Using 59 or an X modifier because the diagnoses differ. Different diagnoses are not enough.
  • Adding a modifier to a pair with NCCI indicator 0. No modifier bypasses it.
  • Putting 59 on an E/M code instead of 25.
  • Expecting 22, 76 or 77 to bypass an edit. They are not NCCI PTP-associated modifiers.

Frequently asked questions

Is this modifier helper free?

Yes. It is free and needs no sign-up. Your answers stay in your browser.

Can modifier 59 go on an E/M code?

No. Modifier 59 should not be appended to an E/M service. A separate E/M on the same day as a procedure uses modifier 25.

Should I use XS or 59?

Use XS when the services were on a separate organ or structure. CMS asks for XE, XP, XS or XU instead of 59 whenever one fits.

Does modifier 25 need a different diagnosis?

No. The E/M service and the procedure can share the same diagnosis. The record must show a significant, separately identifiable E/M service.

Do modifiers 22, 76 or 77 bypass an NCCI edit?

No. They are not NCCI PTP-associated modifiers, so they do not bypass a PTP edit.

Which code gets modifier 59 or XS?

Medicare accepts modifiers 59, XE, XP, XS and XU on either the column 1 or the column 2 code of the edit pair.
