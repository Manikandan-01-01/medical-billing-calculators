Key takeaways

  • Office visits are coded by medical decision making (MDM) or by total time on the date of the visit.
  • The MDM level is the highest level met by 2 of its 3 elements: problems, data and risk.
  • Time thresholds must be met or exceeded: 99213 needs 20 minutes and 99214 needs 30.
  • 99211 has no MDM or time requirement.

How do you determine the E/M level for an office visit?

You pick the level by MDM or by total time, whichever your note supports. Since 2021, history and exam no longer set the level for office visits 99202–99215. The visit still needs a medically appropriate history and exam (AMA CPT 2021 office E/M guidelines).

The E/M calculator works both ways. It shows the code from MDM and the code from time. Then it suggests the higher one, because CPT lets you choose either method.

What are the E/M time thresholds for 2026?

Each office visit code has a minimum total time that must be met or exceeded. CPT replaced the old time ranges with these thresholds in 2024 (AMA CPT Assistant, October 2024). Total time is the billing practitioner’s own time on the date of the encounter, both face-to-face and not.

Count time for preparing to see the patient, the exam, counseling, ordering, referring, documenting and care coordination. Do not count travel, clinical staff time or services you bill separately (AMA, Guidelines Common to All E/M Services: Time). Medicare also says the CPT midpoint rule does not apply here, so you must reach the full time (CMS MLN006764, May 2026).

How is the MDM level calculated?

The MDM level is the highest level where 2 of the 3 elements are met or exceeded. Medical decision making (MDM) is the work of establishing diagnoses, assessing the status of a condition and selecting a management option. Its 3 elements are the problems addressed, the data reviewed and analyzed, and the risk of patient management (AMA CPT E/M guidelines, Table 1: Levels of MDM).

Here is a quick way to read it. Line up the 3 element levels from low to high. The middle one is your MDM level. For example, moderate problems, limited data and moderate risk give moderate MDM, which is 99214 for an established patient.

How do you count data for MDM?

Data has 3 categories. Category 1 counts each unique external note, test result and test order. Category 2 is an independent interpretation of a test. Category 3 is a discussion of management with an external physician or appropriate source (AMA CPT E/M guidelines, Table 1: Levels of MDM).

  • Limited: any 2 notes, results or orders, or an assessment that needs an independent historian.
  • Moderate: 1 of 3 categories. Category 1 here needs any 3 items, and the independent historian counts as one.
  • Extensive: 2 of 3 categories.

Do not count a test you bill separately with its own interpretation code. A test you order and then review counts once, because the review is part of the order (AMA CPT 2021 office E/M guidelines).

When do you use 99211?

99211 is the code for an established patient visit that may not need a physician or other qualified health care professional. Use it when clinical staff do the face-to-face service and the problem is usually minimal. CPT says the level of MDM does not apply to 99211, and time cannot be used to select it either (AMA CPT E/M guidelines, Table 1: Levels of MDM). A blood pressure check by a nurse is a common example.

When do you add 99417 or G2212 for a long visit?

Add a prolonged service code only when you pick the level by time and you pass the top code’s time by 15 minutes or more. CPT uses 99417 with 99205 or 99215. Medicare uses G2212 instead, and its count starts later (CMS MLN006764, Table 3).

Each 99417 unit needs a full 15 minutes past the 99205 or 99215 time (AMA, prolonged services 99417). Do not report G2212 for less than 15 extra minutes past Medicare’s count.

Can you bill G2211 with an office visit?

Yes, for Medicare. G2211 is an add-on for the complexity of an ongoing relationship. You can report it with 99202–99205 and 99211–99215 when you are the continuing focal point for the patient’s care, or you give ongoing care for a single serious or complex condition (CMS MLN006764, May 2026).

Medicare does not pay G2211 when the visit carries modifier 25. The exceptions are a visit on the same day as an annual wellness visit, vaccine administration or another Part B preventive service. Commercial payers set their own rules. To check if a visit needs modifier 25 at all, use the modifier 25 vs 59 helper.

Frequently asked questions

Is this E/M level calculator free?

Yes. It is free and needs no sign-up. Everything runs in your browser. Nothing you enter is sent or saved.

Can I pick the E/M level by time or by MDM?

Yes. For office and outpatient visits 99202 to 99205 and 99212 to 99215, CPT lets you select the level by medical decision making or by total time on the date of the encounter. Use the one your documentation supports best.

How many minutes is a 99214?

When you use time, 99214 needs at least 30 minutes of total practitioner time on the date of the visit. 99213 needs 20 minutes and 99215 needs 40 minutes.

Does history and exam still count for the E/M level?

No. The visit needs a medically appropriate history and exam, but their extent does not set the level. The level comes from MDM or time.

Can 99211 be billed by time?

No. CPT says time and MDM do not apply to 99211. It is for an established patient visit that may not need a physician or other qualified health care professional.

Do commercial payers follow the same rules?

Most payers use the CPT E/M guidelines, but add-on codes differ. Medicare uses G2212 for prolonged office time and G2211 for visit complexity. Many commercial payers use CPT 99417 for prolonged time. Check the payer policy.
